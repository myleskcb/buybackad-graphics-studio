BUYBACK.AD Graphics Studio · engine specification

Buyback Composer

How the studio makes a fresh, good, on-brief ad for any buyback business on every open, from the shop's own facts, without a designer and without repeating itself. Every rule below came from a card you rejected or kept; the why travels with the rule.

1. What it is for

A reseller opens the studio and sees a finished ad for their category, in a palette that is theirs for that session, in a typeface pairing nobody else got today, saying true things about their shop. They can post it, or open it and change anything. Free users get a good one with limits; paid users get the whole range. The graphic must look designed, not generated: nothing overlapping, nothing empty, nothing generic, nothing untrue.

Why an engine and not more templates. Every set so far was hand-cut and reviewed. A library is finite and every card in it is already on someone's feed. The engine turns the reviewed rules into a generator: the same 16 layouts and 56 faces and 16 palettes produce tens of thousands of distinct, valid cards, and each one is checked by the same audit that gated the sets you approved.

2. Inputs: the shop profile

Nothing on a card is invented about the business. The first open shows a three-step sheet; it is short, every field has a sensible default, and the defaults are marked so the owner knows what to change. It is stored once and edited from a single button on the studio bar.

Why a form at all. "We don't want people posting pre-filled generic information or claims that aren't true." Every claim slot on a card maps to a profile field; an unfilled claim slot is filled with a fact the shop did give, never with a placeholder. Competitor lines only render when the owner ticked that they will honour them.

Keeping it un-clunky: three steps, chips before typing, defaults prefilled from category, one "Looks right" button. The studio opens on a finished ad even when the sheet is skipped, using the safe subset (category copy, phone, site) and no claims.

3. The pipeline

One seed drives everything. Given the profile, a category and a seed, the composer is a pure function: same inputs, same card; new seed, new card. Each open draws a seed nobody has used for that shop.

01
Subject The category owns the copy, the product pool and the backdrop pool together. Phones rotates through Apple lines (iPhone ×3, Watch, iPad, MacBook); Samsung and Pixel are their own categories. An iPhone ad never shows another device. Backdrops for a line come only from that line's scenes.
02
Layout One of the 16 approved compositions, drawn by approval rate from the reviews (lowerThird, bubblePop, arcCrown, gradientWave, trustSeal lead). A layout is only eligible for a mode it can carry: challenge mode needs an opener + money-word pair.
03
Mode Sell (SELL YOUR / iPHONE), Buy (WE BUY / COINS), Metal (CASH OUT YOUR / GOLD, CASH IN YOUR), Challenge (WE BEAT / CARVANA'S / TRADE-IN OFFER) at one in three, only if the profile allows it, only with a named competitor from the category's list, never "any offer".
04
Palette One of the approved OKLCH palettes, weighted by approvals; the six newest get a fixed share so the set never settles. Four roles: ground, ink, accent, support. Money band under GOLD is golden, under SILVER is silver. No blur on photographs: one tint, either the ground colour at 42–68% or the accent at 24–30%.
05
Type Display face from the styles that fit the subject (blackletter and serif on metals, arcade and condensed on tech, comic and script on cards); body always a plain grotesque under a wild display; numerals from a mono or condensed set. A headline sentence is one face on every line. Heavy faces carry a word, not a sentence; nothing decorative on a curved line; glow never on condensed.
06
Copy Kicker (TOP BUYER, EZ BUYER, GRAILS WANTED), headline, items line from the profile's models, one selling-point row set, one trust fact, one location line (cities are liked: "helps people see where"), phone, site with a cursor mark. Category jargon on the items line: grails, Moonbreon, Morgans, key dates, Cuban links, president watch.
07
Product A hero cutout from the subject's pool, placed by search at 380/340/320px in spots that clear every text and plate; if nothing clears, no product rather than a hidden one. A layout's own product that lands on text is evicted and re-placed. Above a tall panel with nothing else up there, the product fills 80% of the room.
08
Hygiene passes Boxes hug their text with a fixed margin both ways; side-by-side lines stay inside their plate; headlines stop at 82% width and short of any right column; items lists trim to what the line holds at full size; multi-line text stays in its panel; no text on text (lower rank moves, headline shrinks when nothing can move); no line repeats anywhere on the card; a panel with 70px of empty foot gets a fact.
09
Contrast Every line is measured against the rendered ground under it — the darkest quarter for dark ink, the lightest quarter for light ink — and must reach 4.0:1 at 30px+ or 5.5:1 below; a failing line is re-inked, and if neither ink passes it gets a thin opposite-colour outline (never a glow).
10
Audit The card is rejected and re-seeded if any rule fires: repeat, off-page, text-on-text, tiny (<15px), wide headline, grammar (“a the”), unbounded claim, sparse (edge density <12%). A card the audit passes is the only kind a customer sees.

Even without counting copy rotation, one subject yields more than 16 × 16 × 45 × 5 × 60 ≈ 3.5 million distinct cards. A shop that posts daily for ten years never repeats, and no two shops share a card unless they share a seed, which they cannot.

Why seeds and not random. A seed makes a card reproducible: support can reopen exactly what the customer saw, the audit can be replayed, and "regenerate" is honest — it advances the seed, it does not shuffle the same card.

5. Free and paid

Free

  • One fresh card per day, watermark-free at 1080px
  • Layouts: 6 of 16 (the simplest, still audited)
  • Palettes: the 10 core; faces: the plain styles only
  • Product and backdrop from the library
  • Profile: category, phone, site, two selling points

Paid

  • Unlimited seeds, 2160px, every layout, palette and face style
  • Challenge mode with named competitors and top-payout figures
  • Real photography per subject and generated hero assets
  • Full profile: models, cities, address, claims, review score
  • Saved brand: lock a palette and face pairing as "ours"
Why the free card is still good. "We need to give them something high-quality upfront." The free tier narrows the range, never the quality gate: the same audit, the same contrast rule, the same hygiene. What they cannot get is the range that makes a brand.

8. From lab to studio

Everything above exists today as passes in scripts/retheme_lab.mjs and gates in scripts/audit_set.mjs, run headless over the engine. Productionising is a move, not a rewrite:

  1. compose(profile, category, seed, tier) → template: the lab's retheme() plus its selection tables become one module, with the profile replacing the hard-coded decks and INFO lines.
  2. layout hygiene moves into the engine's build step as a post-pass on the fabric objects, exactly as it runs in paint() now; the measured-contrast repair already exists in app.js and takes the worst-quarter change.
  3. audit(card) → pass/fail runs on the rendered canvas before the card is shown; a fail advances the seed. Budget: three tries, then the safest layout.
  4. assets: cutouts, scenes, the web photographs with their attribution file, and the generated set once a fal key exists, all listed by subject in one manifest with measured luminance.
  5. the sheet: the profile form in section 2, a single JSON document per shop, versioned so a card can say which profile made it.
Why the audit ships too. A generator without its judge is a template pile with extra steps. The audit is what lets the studio promise "never overlapping, never empty, never untrue" on a card no human has seen.
